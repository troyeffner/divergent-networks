## The Core Argument

Most product and organizational work fails at the seams — not because individual teams are incompetent, but because nobody owns the through-line. Each team optimizes their touchpoint in isolation. The journey breaks where departments meet.

The fix isn't better coordination. It's a better artifact: the story.

Write the story first. Then build the technology. Every sentence in the story is a technical and content requirement. Teams that receive the story know what they're building toward. Teams that receive only tasks have nodes without an arc.

## The Full Methodology (what the article covers)

### 1. The Silo Problem
Businesses are organized around functions, not journeys. Marketing owns the email. Product owns the app. Support owns the resolution. Each function optimizes its touchpoint independently. Nobody maps the whole arc. The customer experiences a fractured journey assembled from parts that were never designed to flow together.

This isn't a management failure. It's a representation failure. There is no standard format for "the journey." So it never gets designed.

### 2. Narrative Driven Design — the fix
Tell the story first. Not a user story. Not a job to be done. A narrative — with a character, a journey, and a moment of arrival.

Every story moment decomposes into four layers:
1. **Narrative Step** — what happens, what the person experiences
2. **Direct Touchpoint** — the artifact they interact with
3. **Supporting Technology** — what has to exist to deliver it
4. **Foundational Integration** — how all touchpoints connect into a coherent system

The goal: every moment at "good enough" on equal ground, so the journey flows as a system. One excellent touchpoint surrounded by broken ones is still a broken journey.

### 3. Thread Mode — how this kind of thinking works
Some people see the connective tissue between things. They see arcs, not tasks. The path from A to Z is visible to them before the steps are built.

This is not a skill that transfers automatically. When you hand off tasks, teams get nodes without the arc. They execute correctly and miss the point. The handoff format doesn't have a field for "the thread this belongs to."

Story before spec is how you make the thread explicit. The narrative is the handoff format that crosses silos.

### 4. Fractal Threads — how work actually scales
Follow a thread to a journey point. That journey point spawns sub-threads. Each sub-thread runs its tasks, extracts a reusable pattern, and applies it across the organization. Then you return to the main thread.

This is how you build a platform while solving immediate problems. The thread is the vehicle. The platform is the destination.

### 5. The Research Connection (FFA)
NDD and FFA are the same insight from different angles:
- NDD: write the story → build each touchpoint to serve it
- FFA: research the story (jobs + outcomes) → surface whether the touchpoints are delivering it

Design from story. Build. Research whether the story is being delivered. Refine. That's the closed loop.
